# SC-001 — Revise, interrupt and finish

**Context:** A developer completed A/B. Another session adds unrelated work and participates in a controlled shared-file change. They split downstream C. Assumption: they know the desired outcome, not Loom's internal model.

**Goal:** Finish the Objective with trustworthy reuse and no manual state reconstruction.

**Scenario:** Loom acknowledges the revision and shows retained results, affected results/reviews and next work. Professional realization proceeds; only changed user-owned scope, guarantees, spending or material risk asks approval. After interruption/restart, Loom restores orientation and continues from applicable progress. Failed review retains exact findings; repair is followed by independent re-review. A routine permission boundary is resolved within existing authority, or genuinely new permission is explained once. The authorized outcome is finished and evidenced.

**Failure / edge condition:** Mixed-file authorship cannot be established, notification is missed, or a callback arrives after its attempt changed. Preserve bytes and original observations; name the exact ownership/proof boundary. Authorized consumers retrieve existing answers/evidence directly and record meaningful treatment. Late historical output does not become current proof. No broad rerun, synthetic receipt, transport-only question or cancellation escape substitutes for resolution.

**Observable outcome:** A/B are not repeated solely because unrelated work or presentation changed. A genuinely changed consumed input invalidates affected results/consumers, with reasons and history accessible. No self-approval or false authorship occurs. The whole journey finishes, not merely a merged correction or explanatory status.

**Decisive contrast:** Change an actual consumed input as well as a presentation label to challenge over-permissive reuse. The same result/contract/input/proof tuple has consistent usable/not-usable meaning across preservation and actual downstream use.
