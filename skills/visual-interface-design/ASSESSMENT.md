# visual-interface-design Reviewer Assessment

Reviewer-only domain contract. It does not create Loom authority, product meaning, or proof.

## Review criteria

Reviewer asks whether visual treatment communicates the accepted information/interaction model clearly and consistently:

1. **Hierarchy/reading order** — priority can be inferred from type, spacing, grouping, position, emphasis, and density rather than subjective claims such as “stands out.”
2. **Semantic state distinction** — status, severity, selection, disabled/focus/error/success and other meaningful states are visually distinguishable and have non-color redundancy where meaning would otherwise be lost.
3. **Legibility/density** — type scale, line length, spacing, contrast, and information density fit the content and supported viewport/scale conditions.
4. **Design-system coherence** — tokens, typography, iconography, spacing, component visual language, themes, and exceptions form a system rather than one-off improvisation.
5. **Affordance** — actionable vs static content is visually understandable without contradicting interaction semantics.
6. **Responsive/theme behavior** — hierarchy and meaning survive narrow layouts, zoom/scaling, dark/high-contrast themes where supported.
7. **Motion purpose** — visual motion communicates state, causality, orientation, or confirmation and has reduced-motion treatment where applicable.
8. **Decorative freedom** — decorative color/shape is allowed; it becomes a defect only when it obscures hierarchy, accessibility, brand/system coherence, or semantic meaning.
9. **Boundary discipline** — aesthetics do not invent product behavior or overwrite accepted interaction/information architecture.
