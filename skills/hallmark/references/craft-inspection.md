# Craft inspection without arbitrary taste scoring

Inspect the assembled experience at the level supported by the evidence. Source inspection, rendered observation, interaction, and observation of representative people support different claims.

## Useful questions

- Is the primary action or information legible at the moment the person needs it?
- Do structure, copy, type, spacing, assets, and motion express the accepted direction together?
- Does moving between screens preserve identity, vocabulary, context, and control?
- Do difficult states receive the same care as the happy path?
- Could this be an unrelated product with only its title changed? If so, identify which accepted contextual need or direction is missing; interchangeability alone is not proof of a defect.
- Is an unconventional choice useful here, or merely unfamiliar? Is a conventional choice serving the user, or merely the first available template?

## Record an actionable observation

```text
Intent/reference:
Location and observation method:
Observed mismatch or opportunity:
Consequence for this user/context:
Correction and owner:
Recheck and remaining uncertainty:
```

For example, an accepted direction calls for quiet monitoring. Every successful background update triggers a toast that obscures the active item. The problem is repeated interruption and loss of context, not a personal dislike of toasts. Correct the feedback policy through the existing design owner and recheck the return-to-work journey.

Conversely, slightly different corner rounding with no accepted brand, meaning, accessibility, or task consequence is not a blocking defect. A speculative embellishment belongs in an optional suggestion, not a newly invented acceptance criterion.

Do not record a rendering as inspected unless it was actually available and examined. A static image cannot prove keyboard behavior; source code cannot prove how an animation feels; a prototype using fixture data cannot prove the production capability behind it. Use existing review/validation paths, not a new craft gate.
