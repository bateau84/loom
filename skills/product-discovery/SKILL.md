# Product Discovery

Distinguish what someone asked to build from the problem it should solve, and permission to build from evidence that it will help.

## When useful

Use when the problem, useful scope, intended users, or expected benefit is materially uncertain. Reuse existing observations and accepted decisions. Skip discovery for a clear bounded request with a known problem; do not restart an interview or demand market research for an internal tool.

This method is advisory unless the current assignment gives it a governed home. It creates no execution permission, product authority, gate, required document, or new agent. Existing role and workflow contracts govern decisions, questions, mutations, and completion.

## Method

1. **Ground the problem.** Name the affected person or integration, context, desired outcome, and present obstacle. Separate direct observations, user reports, external evidence, inference, and unknowns. Preserve source and limits; an imagined persona or walkthrough is not observed user behavior.
2. **Separate need from proposed solution.** Explain how the requested feature is expected to remove the obstacle. Where direction is genuinely open, compare a small number of meaningfully different same-goal approaches, including improving an existing path or doing less. Do not reopen fixed choices without material new evidence.
3. **Find the smallest useful outcome.** Describe an end-to-end improvement the intended user could actually use, not a collection of disconnected components. Preserve accepted scope and exclusions. A different goal, weakened guarantee, or material scope/risk change remains with its existing authority owner.
4. **Expose the decisive assumptions.** Identify what could make the approach unnecessary, infeasible, unusable, or too costly. Use `risk-driven-planning` to distinguish evidence needed now from uncertainty that can be carried. Get the cheapest authorized observation, comparison, prototype, or technical probe that could change the decision before elaborating dependent work. A probe involving writes still needs normal execution authority; calling it research does not grant it.
5. **Define useful evidence.** Separate delivery criteria (the feature works) from the expected benefit (the obstacle is reduced). Reuse an available baseline and name what observation would support or contradict the benefit, in which context, and at what useful checkpoint. Propose missing measures rather than silently making them accepted obligations. Do not require analytics, telemetry, exact numeric targets, or external users where a direct observation would suffice.
6. **Return a decision-ready result.** Explain the supported direction, smallest useful outcome, material uncertainty, and next evidence needed. A consciously authorized experiment may proceed with uncertain value; preserve that uncertainty. Do not claim an experiment, interview, probe, or benefit has been observed when it has only been proposed.

## Ownership and handoff

The coordinator keeps the problem and intended benefit visible; this does not transfer specialists' decisions. Human experience stays with its design owner, observable commitments with their specification owner, and structural feasibility with its architecture owner. Research contributes evidence, not accepted product intent.

Reuse the current conversation, permitted handoff, or existing product-intent artifact. Carry only the useful problem/evidence, intended outcome, proposed slice, decisive assumptions, and evidence checkpoint. Do not establish a second authoritative product brief or duplicate an accepted Anchor. `intent-grilling` resolves genuinely user-owned choices; `experience-design` develops the human problem and scenarios; `prototyping` explores unresolved interaction decisions. Discovery connects those methods, not replaces them.

After authorized delivery or an experiment, compare available use evidence with the intended benefit at the agreed checkpoint. Report unsupported benefit honestly without turning it into a new delivery failure or silently expanding the accepted work. Further measurement or changes need their normal authority; this skill does not schedule monitoring.

## Sufficient result

The receiving owner can explain the problem, why this bounded approach is worth trying, which important assumptions remain, and how usefulness could be observed. More interviews or artifacts are not required merely because uncertainty still exists.

Example: a dashboard can accurately show every job without reducing manual supervision. Distinguish the delivery criterion from the benefit hypothesis, compare a smaller intervention path when direction is open, and propose observing whether the operator can handle stalled work without inspecting every session. Do not claim that benefit from a screenshot or the user's approval.
